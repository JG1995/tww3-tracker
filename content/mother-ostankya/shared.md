## The common opening

- **This is a campaign about witchcraft, not a unit restriction puzzle.** Keep useful starting troops and your starting Hag's lore. Recruit an affordable human screen and missile core, then fold forest creatures into it. A template is a destination; an unavailable beast can remain a Warrior or Kossar until its recruitment exists.
- **Choose the starting theatre deliberately.** For the recommended versions of all three plans, stay in Naggaroth. Routes II and III have New World map objectives; Route I can finish in either theatre, so its optional Plesk homecoming is explained under Starting choice. The early dilemma is not a reusable travel button.
- **Finish a local problem before beginning a continental one.** Complete the immediate starting war, inspect the next enemy's army, and protect your recruitment capital. Do not declare war on every named Route III enemy simply because their names are in a future checklist.
- **Plan by readiness, not turn-number benchmarks.** A replenished army, a safe recruiting base and money to replace losses matter more than following an advertised turn-10 rush. Normal/Normal leaves room to learn without deliberately walking into bad battles.
- **Use the right victory panel.** VCO routes and vanilla objectives may coexist on 9.x. Check the VCO route before spending money or crossing the ocean. This atlas never adds a vanilla Hut-location requirement to a VCO route.
- **Theme does not mean no plan.** Each main army has a human escort, a hunter contingent and chosen forest creatures. Their roles develop with the expedition. You need not collect every available unit or replace veterans every time a new tier opens.

## Starting choice: stay in Naggaroth, or answer the homecoming?

- **Recommended: remain in Naggaroth.** Bleak Hold Fortress is the home base for the default plans. Secure the local war, develop the forest roster and decide where the route sends you next. This preserves the geographic logic of Toil & Trouble and The New Frontier.
- **Optional Route I homecoming: Plesk.** Take the early relocation only because you want the Motherland setting. Establish the Eastern Oblast position, negotiate with nearby Kislev factions and pursue nearby hostile armies for witchcraft progress. The five-Hex route itself does not require remaining in the New World.
- **The homecoming is not the easier opening.** Creative Assembly explicitly describes Plesk as the harder start. Keep the opening army together, scout before pushing into nearby Chaos pressure, and favour a support force earlier than the Naggaroth ritual plan would.
- **Landmark location after moving.** The rework allows the Hut at Bleak Hold Fortress or Volksgrad, only one at a time. Take the safe local option and check its actual construction slot. Do not send an army back to Naggaroth solely because an old vanilla guide mentions that landmark.
- **Relocation does not waive objectives.** Moving to Kislev does not turn Route II's Lustrian sites into Cathayan ones or replace Route III's six New World enemies. Those different lists belong to the Realms of Chaos campaign, not relocated Immortal Empires.
- **Already moved and want another route?** Keep your established economy, then build a deliberate New World expedition or use an unlocked, suitable forest teleport to shorten a journey. Secure a destination, recruitment and a return plan before committing. It is a genuine theatre change, not a free route reset.

## Smart Autoresolve • use the real preview, not a vanilla stereotype

- **Treat the preview as your campaign's answer.** Smart Autoresolve changes the evaluation. These templates deliberately cover different matchups, but are not mathematically optimised for an uninspected mod. Compare outcomes in your own save rather than assuming more guns, armour, spells or monsters always wins.
- **Judge the next turn, not just the victory label.** Accept routine victories with manageable damage. Replenish or reinforce before a win that destroys an expensive unit or leaves you exposed to the next army. A living experienced unit is often worth a brief delay.
- **Magic and Incantations are not guaranteed hidden strength.** Do not assume the mod spends your Winds intelligently, fires every bound spell or values Snipe, summons and blessings as a player would. After applying a prebattle Incantation, inspect the preview; do not promise yourself a specific power increase.
- **A manual battle is an option, not a punishment.** Fight important finales or expensive-looking wins manually when practical. Deploy fewer tactical groups, pause after contact and use one spell at a time. You do not need to turn every battle into a micro exercise.
- **The same army is not equally strong under every lord.** A Druzhina does not inherit Ostankya's spell suite or personal forest bonuses. Give supporting armies a narrower job, a Hag and sufficient affordable troops. Do not send a newly recruited copy against her toughest opponents.
- **Quest autoresolve is a separate mod feature.** The button may be enabled by your quest-battle mod, not Smart Autoresolve itself. Keep a pre-finale save and confirm that VCO credits and rewards register. A missing button or missing script credit is not evidence that you need a different army.

The Smart Autoresolve source record marks the evidence boundary: the supplied mod title and description are the only implementation evidence available, no installed calculation was inspected, and no auto-resolve outcome, spell, Incantation, summon or specialist weighting is guaranteed.

## Manual battle • humans, hunters, beasts, witches

- **One formation, three jobs.** Humans make the initial screen; Akshina/Kossars shoot from behind or beside it; the beasts attack units that are already occupied. Keep one or two combat units uncommitted as a response to a breakthrough.
- **The forest is cover, not permission to stop checking shots.** Use clear edges and openings for your shooters and sleds. Woodsman removes relevant movement penalties, but do not assume every projectile travels cleanly through every tree. Inspect a firing lane when a unit refuses to shoot.
- **Spiders and Things are attackers, not Ironbreakers.** Send them into chosen engagements beside the human line or around a flank. Avoid feeding large bodies into braced anti-large units. Pull one endangered group out rather than trying to cycle-charge the entire army.
- **Keep the witches behind the commitment.** Ostankya and Hag sleds can shoot while moving, but the low-micro default is a protected firing position. Move to escape threats or obtain a good spell angle; do not park every caster in an enemy blob.
- **Use a repeatable spell pair.** Slow or weaken the dangerous approach, then damage an engaged enemy cluster. In Hag magic, upgraded spells can change into blessings. Confirm that you are targeting the correct side; your full spellbook is not a requirement to cast everything.
- **Late monsters solve particular problems.** An Incarnate helps with large threats; Things help against infantry; Akshina focus a dangerous target. Spread large entities enough that a single anti-large blob or magical bombardment does not catch all your investment.

**A different job for the supporting army.** A Druzhina is not Ostankya with a different portrait: every lord and hero uses a slot, an additional hero replaces a troop rather than becoming a twenty-first slot, and the supporting commander does not inherit her personal skill bonuses. Keep a Hag for spells, take the relevant support skills and retain enough humans to preserve a coherent formation. Feral bear names can vary by the available recruitment variant; use the feral bear version you actually have in that role, not an assumed unlocked War Bear Rider.

## Shared spending and Devotion discipline

- **Pay for survival first.** Keep enough for replacements and a defensive response. One expedition and an affordable support force are more useful than two showpiece armies that stop all construction.
- **Buy a recruitment milestone, not every military building.** Your first serious military target is the tier-3 Silent Grove for the forest roster and Hags. Use the capital for the later tier-5 Haunted Forest. The animal chain serves different units; it is not the same upgrade path.
- **Make the Hut a funded project.** At tier 3, construct the unique Hut once home is defensible and the treasury can absorb the cost. The archive references a 6,000 base construction cost; check discounts and your live price. Its passive Essence helps every route but is not an army substitute.
- **Let most settlements pay the bills.** Farm and market chains are useful foundations; resources and ports deserve attention where present. Add regional recovery or administration for a reason. Avoid a duplicate recruiting complex in every town.
- **Devotion changes the value of climate.** At least 50 Devotion permits an eligible invocation in owned territory (normal cost 50); at 75 or more Devotion negates climate penalties. Building and preserving enough provincial Devotion can make an unsuitable holding workable, and a province at 90 would fall to 40 after one standard invocation — removing the 75+ protection while a wounded army may need it. Inspect the displayed effect and trend before heavy investment; corruption, plague, climate and Devotion are separate checks.
- **Use the right invocation.** An army invocation prepare an offensive benefit just before the army can use it — Ursun's example includes movement and a battle ability, but its bear-specific damage does not apply to every forest creature. A settlement invocation chooses a provincial defence or economic effect for that province's current job; a winter defence at home does not follow a travelling army to Lustria.
- **Do not spend victory rewards early.** Rewards are a benefit after the route is awarded, not part of your pre-victory income or unit statistics. A completed planner tick is not itself a game victory, and reward receipt is confirmed separately from the victory notification.
- **Keep two ledgers separate: wallet and mission progress.** Pay a known cost, then inspect how much the mission counter moved; the cost and the credit are not assumed equal. Research may cost Essence without adding the same amount to a Hex-spending objective — the unique technologies can still be worthwhile, just not a guaranteed shortcut. A cheaper Hex saves Essence but does not necessarily generate extra progress. Keep a reserve for the next real need: the route is finished by the actual objective, not a spreadsheet total.

Ask whether the next purchase can survive its upkeep: compare the treasury now, the displayed net income (which already includes existing upkeep), the new one-off spending, the additional upkeep, a minimum cash reserve, and a forecast horizon. For an income-upgrade payback, use the marginal income and marginal cost only, and add construction turns; a defensive building, recovery base or recruitment unlock can justify itself without paying back through tax income. This is arithmetic, not a live economy model. **Moving beyond Normal later:** keep the theme, increase cash reserves, scout more carefully and replenish before chaining battles, give the exposed front an extra response force before removing every interesting specialist, and change one difficulty setting at a time — then judge what actually became unreliable.

## The Hag spell primer

- **One Winds pool.** All casters in the army share Winds of Magic. A second Hag supplies another lore, a firing platform and useful support; it does not double your magic budget.
- **Curse versus blessing.** Lore of Hags upgrades can invert a spell into a friendly effect. Read the two versions and save favourite combinations in your notes. The stronger-looking button is not necessarily the right target type.
- **Damage and protection are different jobs.** Use damage magic against valuable dense targets; use protection on the unit actually taking pressure. Thematic casting remains sensible when it buys your army a specific advantage.
- **Map recovery is not battle healing.** Convalescence, Logistician, Man of the Land and a Patriarch's Replenish Troops support recovery between fights. A Hag's blue Protective Charms reduces attrition. A prayer or battle regeneration repairs eligible living combatants during the battle, not a deleted unit after autoresolve.

The Witch's Hut combines unlocked ingredients and trinkets into Incantations: craft first, then apply them to an eligible friendly or enemy unit in the pre-battle interface. These are effect-based recipe goals, not invented fixed ingredient combinations; a crafted Incantation, bound spell or summoned army is not assumed to be valued exactly like its manual-battle effect under Smart Autoresolve.

## A short end-of-turn routine

Before deciding, run the archive's session checklist: army recovery and likely enemy reach are checked; the next Hex or Incantation purpose is clear; forward-province Devotion and climate are checked; VCO progress is checked after relevant actions; and the next operation is written down. The guide does not watch the game — use these as reminders rather than compulsory extra campaign objectives. In the app, the atlas's turn notes, holdings editor and browser-local progress are not present; keep any additional notes outside the app.
